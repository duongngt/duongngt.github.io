In Da Nang, Quan The Am Pagoda sits at the foot of Kim Son, one of the five peaks of the Marble Mountains. Few people know that right next to the pagoda is a cave carved by nature over millions of years. I had passed the Marble Mountains more times than I could count, but it wasn't until one early spring morning that I actually stopped at Kim Son — and was surprised to find such a quiet corner in the middle of the city.

## Kim Son — the peak few people visit

When people mention the Marble Mountains (Ngu Hanh Son), most visitors immediately think of Thuy Son, the largest peak, with its stone steps, pagodas and famous caves. The five mountains are named after the five elements: Metal (Kim), Wood (Moc), Water (Thuy), Fire (Hoa) and Earth (Tho). Kim Son stands a little apart, close to the river, and so it is often left out of tour itineraries.

That quietness is exactly what I love about Kim Son. No crowds, no tour-guide loudspeakers, just birdsong, the sound of the pagoda bell and the faint scent of incense drifting on the breeze.

## Getting to Quan The Am Pagoda

From central Da Nang, I rode toward Hoi An along the coastal road. Reaching the Marble Mountains area, instead of turning into the familiar Thuy Son gate, I kept going a little further and asked for directions to Kim Son. Both sides of the road were lined with stone-carving workshops, the tap-tap of chisels ringing out all morning, rows of stone Buddhas and animal statues standing as if waiting for customers.

Quan The Am Pagoda appeared with its curved tiled roofs, a wide courtyard and cool green trees. I lit a stick of incense, stood quietly for a moment in front of the main hall, then followed a small path beside the pagoda to find the cave.

## The entrance

The entrance is narrow — you have to bend down to get through. The air inside is noticeably cooler, carrying the damp smell of stone and earth. But a few steps later the space opens up, with stalactites hanging like stone curtains.

The feeling is hard to describe: like stepping out of a bright, sunny morning into another world, where time moves more slowly. My footsteps echoed softly off the walls, and without anyone telling me to, I found myself lowering my voice.

## The beauty of the stalactites

Under warm lights the rock walls show every color: earthy brown, moss green, ivory white. In some places the stone is smooth and glossy as if someone had polished it; in others it is rough and jagged, like fossilized waves. Every now and then a small drop of water falls, sparkling in the light.

### Figures in the stone

Locals say many formations resemble the Goddess of Mercy, elephants or turtles — depending on your imagination. I stood for a long time in front of a tall stalactite, trying to find the figure of Quan Am that an incense seller at the gate had told me about. I looked and looked, and only when I shifted where I stood did I see the shape of a figure in a long robe, hands resting gently at her sides. Maybe it was just my imagination, but that moment made the cave feel more alive than any information sign could.

> Stone cannot speak, but everyone who walks into the cave leaves with a story of their own.

## Tips for visiting

If you are planning to visit Kim Son, here are a few things I learned from my trip:

- Wear shoes with good grip; the stone floor is slippery
- Bring a flashlight to see the details
- Go in the morning to avoid the crowds
- Keep quiet — this is a spiritual place
- Dress modestly and respectfully when entering the pagoda
- Bring a small bottle of water, especially on hot days

And a suggested plan for the morning:

1. Set off early and visit Kim Son and Quan The Am Pagoda before the sun gets too high.
2. Allow about an hour to visit the pagoda and explore the cave at a relaxed pace.
3. Stroll through the nearby stone-carving village and watch the artisans at work.
4. If you still have energy, continue to Thuy Son to climb and take in the panoramic view.

Da Nang's weather is most pleasant around spring and early summer, when it isn't too hot yet and there is little rain. In the rainy season the paths can be more slippery, so watch every step.

## What stays with you

I left Kim Son when the sun was already high, carrying a strange sense of calm. There was no grand scenery or "viral" photo, just a small cave, a quiet pagoda and a few minutes of living more slowly.

If you visit the Marble Mountains, don't just climb Thuy Son — save an hour for Kim Son and Quan The Am Cave. Who knows, among those silent stone walls, you might find a figure of your own.
