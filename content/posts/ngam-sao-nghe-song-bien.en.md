Have you ever wanted to step away from the busyness of life and live like Robinson Crusoe on a deserted island with your best friends? We did exactly that — one night camping on the shore of Son Tra. No wifi, no deadlines, no honking horns, just sand, wind, waves and a sky full of stars. This is a small note about that night, along with a few tips for anyone who wants to try it.

## Why Son Tra?

Living in Da Nang, the Son Tra peninsula is almost like a backyard garden. A short ride from the city center and you find green forest hugging the sea, winding mountain roads and little beaches tucked behind rocky outcrops. What I love most about Son Tra is how it feels so close and yet so far: the city is still there, glittering across the bay, but once you are standing between the sound of the waves and the hum of forest insects, all the noise seems to step back.

There were five of us, all friends from high school. Each of us had a different job and a different rhythm of life, and it had been a long time since we had really sat down together. The idea of "one night as Robinson" came up over coffee, and just a week later we had packed our bags and set off.

## Getting ready

You don't need much: a tent, a few tarps, flashlights, water and something to grill. The most important thing is picking a clear, moonless night so the stars shine their brightest.

### What to bring

- Tent and sleeping bags (or an insulated mat to lie on the sand)
- Flashlights, a headlamp and a power bank
- Snacks, and a bit more water than you think you need
- A light jacket, since the sea breeze gets chilly at night
- Mosquito repellent and a small first-aid kit
- A bag to carry your trash home

### Picking the right time

In my experience, the dry months, when there is little rain and the sea is calm, are the most pleasant time to camp in Da Nang. Check the lunar calendar to avoid full-moon nights — the brighter the moon, the fainter the stars. Before you go, look at the weather forecast; if storms or rough seas are expected, postponing is the wisest choice.

## The road to the beach

We set off in the afternoon, once the sun had softened. The road up Son Tra twists and turns, with forested cliffs on one side and deep blue sea on the other. Every so often a bend opens up onto a view of the whole bay, and we had to pull over for a few minutes just to look.

If it's your first time, a few things to keep in mind:

1. Ride your motorbike carefully and keep a moderate speed — the mountain road has plenty of sharp bends.
2. Arrive before dark so you have time to choose a spot and pitch your tent.
3. Camp well above the waterline, away from areas the tide might reach during the night.
4. Ask locals or the people managing the area, and follow their guidance.

By the time our tent was up, the sun had just slipped behind the mountains. The sky turned from orange to purple to deep blue, so quickly that none of us managed to take enough photos.

## When night falls

As the city fades behind you, the waves become the only sound. We lit a small grill, and the smell of grilled squid and sweet potatoes mingled with the salty sea breeze. After dinner, we switched off every flashlight.

It took a few minutes for our eyes to adjust to the dark. Then the stars appeared one by one, growing denser until the whole sky looked dusted with silver. We lay on the sand, watched the Milky Way stretch across the sky, and told each other stories we had never shared — about work, about family, about plans still unfinished. At times we all fell silent for a long while, just listening to the waves lapping steadily at the shore, like the sea's slow breathing.

> Sometimes what you need isn't to go far, but to stop for a while.

## Respecting nature

Son Tra is beautiful because it is still wild, and we want it to stay that way. A few small rules our group always keeps:

- Don't break plants or cut branches for firewood
- Keep the noise down, no loud speakers
- Don't feed wild animals
- Take all your trash home, even fruit peels and tiny scraps of paper

Leaving nothing behind but footprints in the sand — that is the best way to thank a place for giving you a peaceful night.

## What stays with you

The next morning the sun rose over the sea. The first light turned the water gold, and a few round basket boats had already headed out. We sat drinking coffee brewed on a mini stove, looking at each other with sleepy eyes, and everyone was smiling.

A short trip, but enough for everyone to carry a little calm into the new work week. For me, someone who spends all day in front of a screen, that night was a reminder that the world out there is still vast and gentle. If you are feeling worn out, try inviting a few friends, choosing a clear night, and letting the sea tell you a story.
